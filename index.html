STEP 5 · A1

Make sentences — LEGO time! 🧱

A German sentence is like LEGO blocks. Block 1 = who. Block 2 = the doing word (verb). Block 3 = the rest.

Ich1 · WHO
lerne2 · VERB
Deutsch.3 · REST
Die Katze1 · WHO
trinkt2 · VERB
Milch.3 · REST
⭐ The golden rule of German: the verb is ALWAYS block number 2. Never 1, never 3. Remember this one rule and your sentences will be right!

The verb changes a little with the person:

lernen (to learn)trinken (to drink)essen (to eat)
ich (I)ich lerneich trinkeich esse
du (you)du lernstdu trinkstdu isst 😜 (crazy one!)
er / sie (he / she)er lerntsie trinkter isst

🧩 GRAMMAR CHEAT SHEET · A1–B1

Grammar without fear — the 4 rules that matter most

Keep this open while you practise. German grammar looks big, but these four ideas unlock most everyday sentences.

1 · The case changes “the”

Who does it? Nominativ. Who/what receives it? Akkusativ. Only der → den changes in Akkusativ — die and das stay the same. That is a gift!

NominativAkkusativ
derder Hundden Hund
diedie Katzedie Katze
dasdas Brotdas Brot

Example: Der Hund sieht den Mann. — The dog sees the man.

2 · Yesterday = haben / sein + ge-…-t

For most verbs: ich habe + gemacht / gelernt / gespielt. Movement verbs use sein: ich bin gegangen.

lernen →ich habe gelernt
spielen →wir haben gespielt
gehen →er ist gegangen
essen →sie hat gegessen 😜 irregular!

Say it: Gestern habe ich Deutsch gelernt. — Yesterday I learned German.

3 · Asking questions — flip it!

Verb comes first for yes/no questions. Question word first, verb second for open questions.

Sprichst du Deutsch?Do you speak German?
Wo wohnst du?Where do you live?
Wann beginnt der Kurs?When does the course start?
Wie viel kostet das?How much is it?

4 · Saying “not” — nicht vs. kein

Use nicht for verbs/adjectives. Use kein before a noun with no article meaning “no / not a”.

Ich spreche nicht schnell.I don't speak fast.
Ich habe kein Ticket.I have no ticket.
Das ist keine Katze.That is not a cat.

Uli's tip: when in doubt with a noun, try kein first.

❓ BIG QUESTION

"My MSc is in English. Why learn German?"

Good question! You can apply to many English MSc programs with an MOI certificate and no German. But German changes everything after you land:

🎓 More study choices

With German B2–C1, German-taught MSc programs open too — they are also tuition-free and much less crowded.

💶 Better student jobs

Werkstudent (working student) jobs pay your living costs. Most companies ask for German — B1 already helps a lot.

🏠 Easy daily life

Renting a room, going to the doctor, reading office letters (Amt, Anmeldung) — all in German. A2–B1 makes life calm.

❤️ Friends & future

German friends, internships, and later a full-time engineering job and PR — German is the key that opens all these doors.

🪜 THE BIG STAIRS

The 6 levels: A1 → C2

All of Europe uses the same stairs (called CEFR). Here is what you can do on each step, in simple words. Hours = total learning hours from zero, for most people.

A1

Starter 🌱

  • Say hello and talk about yourself
  • Buy things, order food, ask the way
  • Understand slow, simple German
⏱ ~60–150 hours📜 Exam: Goethe A1
A2

Everyday Helper 🌿

  • Talk about family, work, home, plans
  • Handle simple daily jobs alone
  • Write short notes and messages
⏱ ~150–250 hours📜 Exam: Goethe A2
B1

Independent ⭐

  • Manage almost all daily life in Germany
  • Tell stories, give opinions, explain problems
  • The level for many student jobs & Ausbildung
⏱ ~300–450 hours📜 Exam: Goethe / telc B1
B2

Strong Speaker 🌳

  • Talk fluently with native speakers
  • Understand technical texts in your field
  • Work in a German team with confidence
⏱ ~500–700 hours📜 Exam: Goethe / telc B2
C1

University Ready 🎓

  • Study at a German university in German
  • Write long, clear, correct texts
  • Understand lectures, jokes and fast speech
⏱ ~800–1,000 hours📜 Exam: TestDaF / DSH / Goethe C1
C2

Master 🏆

  • Understand everything, almost like a native
  • Say exactly what you mean, always
  • Needed for very few jobs — a bonus, not a must
⏱ ~1,200+ hours📜 Exam: Goethe C2

🎯 Your smart target: B1 before you fly (for life + jobs), then grow to B2–C1 in Germany. That plan is realistic in about a year of daily study.

📜 EXAM & MONEY

The exam — and what things cost

Simple facts, no fear. The exam is just 4 small tests. And the money side is clear if you plan early.

📝 The exam has 4 parts

📖 LesenReading — read texts, answer questions
👂 HörenListening — listen, answer questions
✍️ SchreibenWriting — write a letter or text
🗣️ SprechenSpeaking — talk with a partner & examiner

You need about 60% to pass. Practice with free model tests (Modellsatz) from Goethe before the real exam — same shape, no surprises.

Which certificate?

Goethe-Zertifikat — the most famous, accepted everywhere.
telc — also accepted, sometimes cheaper.
TestDaF / DSH — only if you apply for a German-taught degree (you need C1 level).
For an English-taught MSc, universities usually do not ask for a German certificate — German is your life-and-job superpower, not an admission rule.

🚫 DON'T DO THIS

5 mistakes that slow you down

1

Learning nouns without der/die/das

„Hund" alone is half a word. Always learn „der Hund" — with its color. Fixing this later is painful.

2

Only apps, no speaking

Apps build knowledge; only talking builds speaking. Add a tandem partner from A2 — even 30 minutes a week.

3

Studying only on weekends

Your brain forgets fast. 30 minutes every day beats 5 hours on Friday. Never miss a day — even 10 minutes counts.

4

Translating word by word from English or Bangla

German has its own order (verb = block 2!). Think in German blocks, not in translated words.

5

Being shy about mistakes

Germans are happy when you try. A sentence with 2 mistakes still works. Silence teaches nothing — talk, err, laugh, learn!

❓ QUESTIONS

Things everybody asks Uli

Is German very hard?

No — it is logical. Words are built like LEGO: „Krankenhaus" = sick-house = hospital. The grammar has rules, and the rules mostly work. English speakers find the start friendly: many words are cousins (Wasser = water, Brot = bread, Haus = house).

I know ZERO German. Can I really start here?

Yes — this page was made for exactly you. Step 1 is the alphabet. Do the 8 steps in order, a little every day, and you will be at A1 level and ready for the free DW course.

How long to reach B1?

With 2 hours a day, about 6–8 months for most learners. Use the plan maker above with your own hours — it shows your personal timeline.

Do I need German for an English-taught MSc in Germany?

For admission, usually no — many universities accept an MOI certificate instead of IELTS and ask for no German. But for student jobs, daily life, internships and staying in Germany after your MSc, German B1–B2 is a huge advantage. Start now, while you have time.

Free self-study or a paid course — which is better?

Both work. Self-study with DW Nicos Weg + Anki is free and proven — it needs self-discipline. A course (Goethe Dhaka or a local online batch) gives a teacher, classmates and exam training. Many learners mix: self-study A1–A2 free, then a course for B1 exam preparation.

When should I book the Goethe exam?

Book as soon as you can pass a full model test at home. In Dhaka, seats fill quickly and Goethe's own students get early places in some months — check dates on the official exam page and register early.
